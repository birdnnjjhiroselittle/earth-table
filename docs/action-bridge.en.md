# The Action Bridge: Hunt & Harvest

**English** | [日本語](action-bridge.md)

**Ancient fun to rescue modern waste. The more you play, the healthier you and your town become.**

## 1. Why it's fun

In prehistoric times, the strongest went hunting while others gathered nuts and fruit. Searching, hunting, making and sharing were directly tied to survival, so our brains evolved to find them **enjoyable**. That is why fishing, foraging, tool-making and cooking are still loved as hobbies today.

Agriculture and industrialization brought an age of plenty, and those pleasures moved into hobbies and video games.
Hunt & Harvest **reconnects that fun to real food.**

## 2. Gameplay mapped to reality

| Ancient instinct | Gameplay | What happens in reality | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gathering** | "Harvest spots" appear on the map; walk to collect them | Rescue boxed meals and near-date food before stores close | The food, discounts |
| **Hunting** | Rare prey, time-limited events | Use wild deer and boar culled for crop protection, and underused fish | Meat and fish |
| **Big-game hunt** | 30-player "harvest raid" | Help short-staffed farms with the harvest | Imperfect-looking produce, a share of the crop |
| **Tool making** | Crafting by combining ingredients | "Use-it-up quests" for cooking with leftovers from the home fridge | Levels, points |
| **Growing** | Tend a farm | Home gardens and community plots | The harvest |
| **Sharing** | Delivery quests to allies | Bring food to children's cafeterias and people in need | Local points, community thanks |
| **Passing on knowledge** | Complete the field guide, become a master spotter | Photograph produce to help train freshness-detection AI | Titles, points |

## 3. Why players can earn real food

Food waste is value that cost money and labor to produce but never benefited anyone. Rescue it, and that value can be shared.

| Who pays | What they gain | Player reward |
|---|---|---|
| Stores | Lower disposal costs; unsold stock becomes revenue | The rescued food, discounts |
| Farms | Harvest labor | Imperfect-looking produce |
| Municipalities | Lower waste-processing costs | Local points (funded from part of the savings) |
| Health insurers and employers | Less disease, lower medical costs | Points for activity and vegetable intake |
| Regions hit by crop-raiding wildlife | Less crop damage | Wild game meat |

### Rewards = shadow prices

When EARTH TABLE's Earth Twin shows that a town has surplus tomatoes, the reward for tomato-rescue quests goes up.
**The more surplus a town has of something, the bigger the reward for rescuing it.** The game's rewards are themselves the signal for planetary optimization.

## 4. How it makes people healthier

- **Walk to gather:** harvest spots and prey appear a little way off, so people naturally walk
- **Your character's stamina = your real meals:** vegetables restore it; too much salt slows you down a little. Never shames body shape or weight
- **Level up by cooking:** the more you cook, the more skilled you get and the more dishes you unlock
- **Seasonal events = seasonal food:** spring wild greens, autumn harvests; eating what's in season earns special rewards

## 5. How it helps end suffering

- **People in need take part as players:** they earn food through play instead of receiving charity, which protects their dignity
- **Quests for people who can't get around:** home-based tasks (sorting photos, writing recipes, wellness phone calls) so older and disabled people can take part
- **Deliveries double as check-ins:** sharing food with elderly people who live alone also confirms they're doing well
- **Guilds (town teams):** cooperation over competition. The whole town works toward a shared goal ("cut waste by X kg this month")

## 6. Safeguards

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Stores over-order because "players will rescue it anyway" | Stores whose surplus grows earn lower rewards. Reducing surplus through forecasting always comes first |
| Addiction and pressure to pay | No loot boxes, no paid items. Daily limits; breaks are encouraged |
| Accidents from playing while walking | Nothing is shown while moving. AR glasses display only in safe places |
| Food safety | Hand-offs follow hygiene rules and guidelines. Temperature-sensitive items have conditions |
| Treating players as cheap labor or taking jobs | Farm help is framed as an experience, not employment (legal review needed), and never displaces existing jobs |
| Legal compliance | Review against prize/promotion law, gambling law, labor law and food sanitation law in each country |

## 7. Precedents

- **Pokémon GO:** a game that got people around the world walking outdoors
- **Walking-reward apps** (e.g., Coke ON Walk in Japan): reaching step goals earns stamps that can be exchanged for drinks
- **Food-rescue apps:** connect stores and buyers for food that would otherwise go unsold

Hunt & Harvest links these to three goals at once: **less food waste, better health, and support for people in need.**

## 8. Roadmap

1. Pilot as a smartphone map game with one supermarket, a few farms and one children's cafeteria
2. Connect to home fridges and add use-it-up quests and cooking
3. Partner with farms and hunters' associations to add harvest raids and wild game
4. Support AR glasses and earbuds ("There's a harvest spot nearby")
5. Connect to EARTH TABLE's Earth Twin so quests are triggered by its signals
